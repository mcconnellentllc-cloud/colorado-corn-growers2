Get Into Your USDA Account

Posted October 20, 2026

There is one piece of paperwork that makes every future program easier, and almost nobody does it until they need it. Linking a Login.gov account to your USDA customer record takes a few minutes, costs nothing, and is the difference between a pre-filled application and a blank form at the county office.

USDA said exactly that when it announced Harvest Fuel Relief. Rates are not out and the application window is not open, so this is the only useful thing anyone can do about that program today. It is also what gets you into ARC/PLC enrollment, your farm records and your payment history without a drive to town.

What the Account Actually Gets You

An authenticated farmers.gov account is not a brochure login. Inside it you can see your farm records and maps, view your FSA payment history, check conservation practices and loan status, sign documents, and apply for programs online instead of by appointment.

That last one matters more each year. Programs increasingly open online first, and the producers already set up are the ones who are not waiting on a county appointment in a week when every other producer wants the same slot.

Three Steps, in This Order

The order matters. Most of the frustration people report comes from doing step two before step one.

Step 1. Make sure USDA has a customer record for you. A customer record is what USDA already holds on you — name, address, phone, and any legal representative relationships. If you have done business with FSA you almost certainly have one, but it may be out of date, and an out-of-date record is the usual reason the link fails later. Call or visit your local USDA Service Center and ask them to confirm it exists and that the details are current. Do this first.

Step 2. Create a Login.gov account and verify your identity. Login.gov is the government-wide sign-in service, not a USDA system, and the same account works across other federal agencies. Verification can be done online, which USDA recommends, or in person. Requirements change, so check login.gov/help for what is accepted today rather than taking anyone’s word for it, including ours.

Step 3. Link it at farmers.gov. Go to farmers.gov/account, click Log In, choose Customer on the landing page, and then select the Login.gov option. The system will try to connect your Login.gov account to your USDA customer record automatically.

When the Record Will Not Link

This happens to people who have farmed with FSA for thirty years, so do not assume you have done something wrong. USDA says plainly that in some circumstances the system cannot connect the two even when you have worked with USDA before.

When that happens you are routed to a page offering Start Customer Data Worksheet or Check for Record. Use Check for Record first. If that fails, the worksheet is how you give USDA what it needs to match you, and then the Service Center finishes it from their side. A phone call to the county office at that point saves more time than another hour of clicking.

If You Still Use eAuth

The old eAuth username and password are being retired for this purpose. If that is still how you sign in to farmers.gov, you will have to switch to Login.gov. Doing it now, on a quiet afternoon, is considerably better than doing it on the day a sign-up opens.

What Is Coming: One Farmer, One File

USDA announced an initiative in February 2026 called One Farmer, One File — a single record that follows a producer across FSA, NRCS and RMA, with one sign-in for enrolling in programs at more than one agency. The idea is that you stop re-supplying the same information to three agencies that already have it.

USDA anticipates completing that work in 2028, so it is a direction of travel rather than something to wait for. The Login.gov account you set up today is the front door to it either way.

Where to Get Help

The two kinds of problem have two different places to go, and sending the wrong one to the wrong place is how afternoons disappear.

Trouble creating or verifying the Login.gov account itself — that is Login.gov, not USDA. Start at login.gov/help.

Trouble with your USDA record, or the link between them — that is your local USDA Service Center. They can see your customer record; Login.gov support cannot.
