Ask of the Board

Grow the Facebook Reach

We are asking every director to spend twenty minutes on the association’s Facebook account adding Colorado ag producers they know. Not a campaign — just your own contacts, the growers and ag businesses you already deal with.

Here is why it is worth your twenty minutes. Reach is the cheapest advocacy tool this association has. When the December 11 enrollment deadline is about to catch somebody out, or Harvest Fuel Relief rates finally land, the number of growers who actually see it is decided weeks earlier by how many people are connected to us. A post reaches the people already there and nobody else. Every name added now is a grower who gets the warning next time.

It is also what makes the sponsorships worth money. We are asking banks, fuel and energy companies, equipment and irrigation dealers for $5,000 and $10,000 a year. The first thing a sponsor asks is how many producers we reach. That number is one we build ourselves, one contact at a time, and right now it is the weakest part of the pitch.

Credentials are not on this page, and will not be. This site is served from a public repository and this page opens to anyone with the link — a password written here is a password published. Ask the office for the Facebook login directly, or get it from the board’s password manager.
Two practical notes. Facebook flags accounts that are signed into from many places at once and can lock them, so coordinate with the office rather than all piling in on the same evening — and it is worth asking whether directors should be added as Page roles on their own accounts instead of sharing one login, which is both safer and what Meta’s terms expect. Add producers you actually know. Bulk-adding strangers is the fastest way to get the account restricted, which would cost us the reach we are trying to build.

Ask of the Board

Record a Short Video

We are asking every director to record 60 to 90 seconds on their phone about what Rep. Boebert’s work has meant for their operation. Not a speech. A grower standing in their own shop or at the edge of their own field, saying plainly what changed and what still needs doing.

This matters because the association cannot say the one thing that actually lands. We can write that a disaster programme reached this state. Only you can say it kept your operation whole in a year you thought it would not be. People believe a farmer in a seed cap. They scroll past a press release.

Shooting it

  • Hold the phone sideways — landscape, not upright. Vertical video cannot be used anywhere but a phone screen.
  • Light on your face, not behind you. Standing in a doorway with the sun at your back makes you a silhouette.
  • Get close, or get out of the wind. Phone microphones lose to a breeze every time. A cab or a shop is better than an open field.
  • One take is fine. A stumble reads as honest. A polished read does not.
  • Send it as-is. Do not trim it, and do not add music.

What to say, in order

  1. Who you are. Name, the county you farm in, and what you grow.
  2. What the problem was. The drought year, the input bill, the programme that stopped at the state line.
  3. What she did about it. One thing, concrete. See below.
  4. What it meant on your place. This is the part nobody else can say.
  5. What you want next. Finish the job — a permanent disaster title, a farm bill that moves.

What she has actually done — use these

She brought growers to the table at Lamar. Rep. Boebert convened an agriculture round table at the Cow Palace and sat through the case Colorado producers came to make, rather than delivering remarks and leaving. Local coverage of the round table is the source if anyone asks.
She carried the permanent disaster title into Washington. The argument is that disaster aid should be written into the farm bill rather than passed ad hoc every time a year goes bad — reaching producers who take a real loss without propping up producers who do not. That is a hard case to make in Washington, and the 4th District carries the eastern plains and most of Colorado’s corn ground, so it is the right member to be making it.
Everything else has to come from you. Those two are what this association has sourced and stands behind. They are deliberately the only ones listed. If she has done something for your operation that is not on that list, that is the most valuable thing in your video — say it, and tell the office so we can source it and add it to the record.
Two rules, and they matter. First, do not put a number, a date or a dollar figure in your video that you cannot point to a source for. One wrong figure gets quoted back at us for a year. Say “it made the difference” rather than guessing at a percentage. Second, keep the two things separate: describing what she did in office is the public record and anyone can speak to it, while asking people to vote for her is the board’s endorsement. The board did vote to pledge this association’s support, so either is fair — just say which one you are doing instead of blurring them together.
Revenue Programme

Member Packets

Paid how-to packets for the general membership. $50 each, one-time. Status of each packet is below. Nothing here has been offered for sale yet.

1. Pass-Through Entities and Payment Limits Draft — unverified

How USDA’s 2026 rule treats qualifying LLCs and S-corps as pass-through entities, so the payment limit is multiplied by the number of members who are actively engaged, rather than the single limit those entities carried before. Covers what “actively engaged” now requires, AGI tested at member level, and the ways a wrong structure costs eligibility.

Before it can be sold: somebody has to read 7 CFR 1400 as amended and confirm the figures against the rule itself. The draft was written from secondary summaries. Also open: the 2026 farm operating plan deadline appears to have passed on September 15, so confirm with FSA what late filers can do — that answer changes what the packet is worth.
Programme go-live Blocked

Waiting on the written USDA ethics opinion before anything is offered for sale, because a director sits on the State FSA Committee. Revenue going to the association rather than to individuals is the favourable fact, and it should be the first thing the request states.

Also undecided: how membership gets verified at checkout. Membership runs through NCGA, so CCGA may not hold a roster of its own. Simplest start is a code in the member newsletter.
Later packets Ideas

ARC against PLC election mechanics. Base acre reconstitution. Disaster programme stacking. Each one its own packet, each sourced to the rule it describes.

Why the packet text is not on this page. This site is served from a public GitHub repository, so anything committed here can be read by anyone and stays in the history permanently. A packet published here is a packet given away. Drafts stay off the repo until there is something that checks who is asking — the magic-link portal at /board/ once the Worker is deployed, or the storefront that sells them.
