Should CCGA sign on to the amicus brief in the Dryland Partners case?

A district judge threw out Xcel's attempt to condemn an easement across a Cheyenne County farm. Xcel has asked the Colorado Supreme Court to step in early. A brief supporting the landowner is reportedly already written, and we have been asked to add our name.

The decision

What we are being asked

Brian Dickey of Tenfold Farms has asked CCGA to join an amicus brief supporting Dryland. Because the brief is already drafted, there is no writing for us to do, no word limit to manage, and almost certainly no counsel-of-record duty. But an amicus signs the whole brief. There is no joining as to Parts I and III only — every argument in it is argued in CCGA's name.

So the question is not "should we participate." It is "do we want our name on these specific words" — and nobody on this board has read them. We do not have the brief, do not know who drafted it, and do not know the filing date.

Proposed motion

Authorize signing on, contingent on a board read of the actual brief — and open the 2027 legislative file regardless.

The reason to be on this brief is specific: paragraph 25 of the order found that condemnation at $19,886 would reset the valuation for every future easement negotiation at that substation. That is an agricultural-economics point no party is positioned to make, and it belongs to a growers' association. If the existing brief does not make it, our signature adds a name to the caption and nothing else — and asking for it to be added is the fair price of joining.

The reason for caution is governance, not law: more than a hundred landowners signed easements for these same tie-lines, many of them growers. A brief speaks for all our members, and they are not uniformly on Dryland's side.

  1. Read it first. Circulate the brief to the officers and a board attorney. No signing on a description of a brief.
  2. Check what it leans on. If it rests its weight on the mining-limitation holding (see fold below), the board should know it is signing the most reversible part of the ruling.
  3. Ask for the paragraph 25 point if it is not already there.
  4. Confirm no cost-share and no filing duty on our end, and get the intended filing date.

If the brief cannot be read in time, decline and issue a board resolution instead. Separately and unconditionally: open the 2027 legislative file on HB26-1278 — Senator Pelton represents Cheyenne Wells and carried it. That work is squarely ours and carries none of the membership risk.

What the court held — four independent grounds

Novel
No statutory authority to condemn
Read C.R.S. § 38-4-103 as limited to mines and mining claims. No Colorado appellate court has so held. The most likely ground to be reversed.
Strong
A separate statute bars it outright
C.R.S. § 40-2-125, adopted by voters in 2004, bars condemnation to site renewable generation facilities. The court refused to let PSCo escape it by relabeling the line.
Contestable
No public use
The power moves to the Front Range; the gen-ties are "sole use facilities" under PSCo's own agreements. Strains against Carousel Farms.
Strong
Not necessary, and negotiated in bad faith
PSCo approved a Section 24 corridor after years of collaboration, then withdrew and moved on Sections 18 and 19 — ground it knew was already encumbered. Offer: $19,886.36.

Xcel must beat all four. Three rest on findings about what this utility did on this ground — including a finding that its siting manager's testimony was incredible. Appellate courts do not re-weigh credibility findings.

Three weaknesses the board should know about

The mining limitation is novel. § 38-4-103 has been on the books since 1907 and utilities have assumed broader reach. This is the most cert-worthy issue in the order — and, unhelpfully, the ground the court reached first.

Public use strains against Carousel Farms, which permits substantial private benefit so long as the purpose is essentially public. The court itself conceded one Tanner factor favors PSCo.

The permit-feasibility argument already lost below, on Shaklee. If the brief revives it, that is a flag.

And the politics run against us: the Colorado Energy Office — a state agency — and Tri-State are weighing briefs for Xcel, framing this as 1,000+ MW of finished wind stranded behind 550 feet of ground.

For and against

For

Reasons to sign

  • We have an argument nobody else can make. What a sub-$20,000 condemnation comparable does to every producer negotiating with a wind or solar developer. That is the amicus function exactly.
  • The field is one-sided. A state agency and Tri-State for Xcel; nothing yet for the landowner.
  • The mechanism generalizes. If relabeling a gen-tie as transmission works, any developer can convert a negotiated easement market into a condemnation at will.
  • It costs us almost nothing now. No drafting, no word limit, no filing — the objection that dominated the original version of this memo is gone.
  • The legislature is closed to us. HB26-1278 died in committee. There is no bill to lobby this session.
Against

Reasons to decline

  • Our members are not on one side. 100+ landowners signed easements for these same tie-lines. Many are growers. Some may read our brief as arguing their deals were struck under a broken system.
  • We do not control a word of it. Signing someone else's brief means accepting arguments we would not have chosen — including, possibly, the weak one.
  • We may be attaching our name to a shaky holding. If the Supreme Court reverses, CCGA is publicly on the losing side of a property-rights case, right as a 2027 bill is drafted.
  • Paragraph 25 cuts both ways. Leaned on too hard, it reads as an association arguing for higher easement prices rather than for property rights. Xcel's counsel will make exactly that read.
  • Lighter instruments exist. A board resolution, a letter to Dryland, testimony on a 2027 bill.

Before the board votes — four calls to make

  1. 01Get the brief itself. From Brian Dickey or Bradley Haight, Dryland's counsel. Nothing else can be decided without it.
  2. 02Who drafted it, and when does it file? The real cutoff is drafting counsel's filing date, not the rule's deadline — the motion for leave names the amici, so we must be on before it goes.
  3. 03Any cost-share or filing duty for us? Confirm CCGA carries no counsel-of-record obligation. If Dryland's own counsel wrote the brief, that gets disclosed and a court discounts it accordingly.
  4. 04Does Dryland's counsel even want amici? Some do not. Ask before assuming.

Also outstanding: the Sierra Club brief Brian referenced never arrived and did not surface in research — it may be from the Elbert County Power Pathway matter rather than this one. Both of Brian's emails came through without attachments; ask him to resend.
