Tools for Colorado Growers

Updated October 26, 2026

The things a Colorado corn grower actually needs, in one place: the decision aids worth your time, the accounts that unlock everything else, and the deadlines that decide whether a payment reaches your operation.

We have tried to keep this short. Everything on this page is either free, run by people who answer the phone, or both — and where a tool is built on national averages, we say so, because a national average is not a Colorado answer.

Work Your Own Numbers

ARC/PLC Decision Tool

Agricultural and Food Policy Center · Texas A&M · Free

This is the one to use before December 11. It runs your own base acres and your own program yields rather than handing you a national average, which matters more here than almost anywhere — Colorado’s PLC yields sit below the national average because so much of our corn is dryland.

The current version carries a 2026 FSA ARC/PLC Expected Payments simulation. Rather than giving you one number, it estimates the probability of different payment levels across 500 combinations of market prices and county yields. That is the honest way to look at a decision you have to make before you know the weather or the market.

AFPC builds the representative farm models Congress leans on when it writes this policy, so this is not a third-party guess at the rules. It is from the people who help write the analysis behind them.

Open the decision tool →
There is a staffed helpdesk. If you get stuck on your own numbers, call 844-229-7373 and a person will walk you through it. Very few federal program tools come with that, and it is worth using.

Deadlines and Programs Running Now

ARC/PLC Election and Enrollment

Closes December 11, 2026

Enrolling is required even if you are changing nothing. An unenrolled farm receives no 2026 payment — that is USDA’s own wording, not our warning.

Harvest Fuel Relief

$8 billion · Rates expected November

One-time payments for the 2026 crop year, aimed at what diesel did to cost of production. No payment rate and no application window yet, so anyone quoting you a per-acre figure is guessing. One thing is worth doing today, and it is the USDA account above.

Dyed Diesel on the Road

Executive order, October 5

Off-road dyed diesel can run on the highway and the 24.4 cent federal tax is deferred through year end. Deferred is not forgiven, and Colorado’s own 20.5 cent special fuel tax is a separate question the order does not reach.

When USDA’s Numbers Move

NASS correction, October 23

NASS corrected its September corn data after a processing error reached two states and the national forecast. Worth knowing because the Harvest Fuel Relief formula takes WASDE yields as an input, and those rates land in November.

CLIP — Crop & Livestock Income Protection

Federally subsidised · Co-developed by CCGA

An umbrella revenue option that works alongside an existing Revenue Protection policy, letting diversified operations elect higher coverage at better rates by leaning on the risk diversification of several commodities. CCGA helped conceive it with Texas Corn Producers and AgriLogic.
