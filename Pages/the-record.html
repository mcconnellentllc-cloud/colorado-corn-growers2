The Record

Posted September 25, 2026 · Updated as the record changes

Growers ask us all the time who is actually working on this. Here is the answer, kept the only way it is worth keeping: by what people did in office, with a source attached.

How This Page Works

Three rules, so nobody has to wonder.

  • An entry is an action taken in office — a bill introduced or cosponsored, a vote cast, a hearing held, testimony given. Not a statement of support, and not a kind word at a meeting.
  • The record runs both ways. Where someone we work with voted against something Colorado corn wanted, that is on the page too. A list that only flatters is not a record, it is a thank-you note.
  • Officials are listed by what they did, not by party, and they are not ranked. Several of the people below appear on ballots. CCGA takes no position on candidates or elections and nothing here is an endorsement.

Colorado’s Delegation

Rep. Lauren Boebert (R), 4th District. The 4th takes in the eastern plains and most of Colorado’s corn ground. She hosted the agriculture round table at the Cow Palace in Lamar, where growers made the case directly for a permanent disaster program written into the farm bill — aid that reaches producers who take a loss without propping up producers who do not.

Sen. Michael Bennet (D). A longstanding member of the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition and Forestry, where he chairs the Subcommittee on Conservation, Climate, Forestry and Natural Resources, and a negotiator on two bipartisan farm bills. He has run repeated farm bill listening sessions across Colorado. On September 16 he voted against the Agricultural Act of 2026 in committee, which was reported out 12 to 11. Both of those facts belong on the same page: a senator who knows the file well and a vote that went the other way from where this association sits on getting a bill moved.

The Colorado General Assembly

Sen. Cleave Simpson (R). A fourth-generation San Luis Valley farmer and rancher and general manager of the Rio Grande Water Conservation District, which is to say he does the water work for a living rather than reading about it. He sits on the Senate Agriculture and Natural Resources Committee, the committee that hears Colorado’s pesticide and seed bills, and he testified before the United States Senate Agriculture Committee when it held its farm bill field hearing in Colorado.

Outside Colorado

Corn policy is not written in Colorado. These are the offices whose decisions land on our ground, and where our affiliation with SWCA gets us heard.

Sen. John Boozman (R–Arkansas), Chairman, Senate Agriculture Committee. Authored the Agricultural Act of 2026 and moved it out of committee on September 16 after an August attempt fell short. For corn growers the consequential piece inside it is permanent, year-round, nationwide E15 — ethanol demand written into law instead of granted one summer at a time by emergency waiver.

Sen. John Hoeven (R–North Dakota). Cosponsored legislation to allow year-round E15 sales and end the annual waiver scramble.

Rep. G.T. Thompson (R–Pennsylvania), Chairman, House Agriculture Committee. Introduced H.R. 9535, the Securing Agriculture’s Workforce Act of 2026 — the first statutory reform of the H-2A program in forty years. It removes the seasonal requirement that has kept year-round operations out of the program, which is the provision that reaches Colorado dairies and feedyards, and feedyards are corn customers.

Who Is Not on This Page, and Why

Candidates are not listed here, including candidates whose work we know and respect. The page is a record of actions taken in office, and a candidate has none to record. Listing one anyway, in the weeks before an election, would make this an endorsement no matter what the heading said — and CCGA does not endorse.

That is a rule about the page, not a judgment about any person on or off it. After an election, anyone who takes office starts building a record like everybody else, and the record goes here.

Tell Us What We Have Missed

This page is maintained, not carved. If an official has acted on something that matters to Colorado corn — a vote, a bill, a letter, a hearing — send it to office@cologrowers.com with something we can cite, and it goes up. That standard applies to everyone equally.

Committee assignments, votes and bill numbers are from the public record. Several officials named here appear on ballots; CCGA takes no position on candidates or elections, and nothing on this page is an endorsement or a recommendation to vote for or against anyone.
